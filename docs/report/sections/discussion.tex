\section{Limitations and Future Scope}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One main GPU, under WSL2.} The selector was trained and almost every number measured on one RTX~4050
  Laptop. Clocks and thermals vary, which we contain with medians, shuffled orders and AC power but cannot remove.
  WSL2 also changes the semantics we study: managed memory never migrates, so paper~1's unified-memory experiments
  cannot be reproduced, and host-side latencies of tens of microseconds weigh on small SpMVs and on G3.
\item \textbf{Few large held-out matrices.} Only \stat{p3-heldout-large} held-out samples have at least $10^6$
  nonzeros, so the ``never lose on large matrices'' half of G2 rests mostly on cross-validation.
\item \textbf{G3 is not met} (\stat{p3-g3-median} SpMVs median in the final run, 1.97 in September): cheap for large
  matrices, expensive for small ones, where the feature gate simply skips selection. The increase came with a driver
  update, as a fixed host-side cost per call.
\item \textbf{G6 is not met.} On $A\cdot P$ RASP reaches \stat{p4-g6-AxP}\x, just short of 1.3\x, because
  estimation and planning take \stat{p4-overhead-pct}\% of the time. On $R\cdot A$ (\stat{p4-g6-RxA}\x) the cost model
  over-partitions $A$ and $C$ once $B$ must be split, and re-planning cannot repair the split. On this machine, overlapped Algorithm~4 is the better schedule for both
  products.
\item \textbf{SpGEMM engine.} cuSPARSE's SpGEMM does not report its workspace in advance and blocks the host in its
  size queries; we learn the workspace size and plan around the stalls. Our partial-product sum uses one thread per
  row, which suits multigrid products but not power-law graphs.
\item \textbf{Workloads.} Out-of-core results cover paper~1's multigrid products on Laplace3D. Graph products
  ($L\cdot L$ for triangle counting) are supported by the benchmark but were not evaluated.
\item \textbf{Budget emulation.} As in paper~1, the fast-memory budget emulates a smaller GPU; the operands live in
  pinned host memory and the size estimate runs on the host CPU.
\item \textbf{CPU comparison.} The OpenMP scaling study ran on a hybrid-core i5-1235U whose sequential baseline drifts
  by about $\pm10\%$ between runs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Future scope.} The most direct improvement is to the SpGEMM planner: penalise the number of tiles in the
cost model and allow re-partitioning of $B$ blocks, so that RASP stops losing to overlapped Algorithm~4 on $R\cdot A$.
A custom hash SpGEMM with a column-range filter, as in \texttt{kkmem}, would remove cuSPARSE's host round trips and the
full-height row pointers of $B$ blocks, and would make graph workloads such as triangle counting worth evaluating.
Retraining the selector on a data-centre GPU (A100 or H100) and on native Linux would show how well its features
transfer across architectures, and would let us measure unified-memory oversubscription, which WSL2 prevents. On the
solver side, fusing CG's vector updates and reductions into fewer kernels would attack the larger part of each
iteration, since SpMV is only \stat{p5-cg-spmv-share}\% of a solve. Finally, the same planning approach extends naturally to several GPUs or to an MPI cluster, where
the link to optimise around is the network rather than PCIe.
